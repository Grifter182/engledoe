Funeral cover

How much funeral cover
does a family need?

Work the number up from a real quote, not down from a round figure. Then check it covers every life your family would be expected to bury.

Insure the funeral you would actually hold, for every life you would actually bury. That is the whole method. R50 000 is not an answer, it is a round number somebody put on an advertisement. The answer comes from a written quote for a specific funeral, plus the costs that never appear on a funeral home’s invoice.

Start with a real quote, not an average

National averages are a reasonable sanity check and a poor planning tool. What a funeral costs depends on burial or cremation, on the cemetery, on how many people will be fed, and on choices your family has probably already made in their own minds without ever saying them out loud.

So begin at the other end. Telephone us, or any funeral home you trust, and describe the funeral you have in mind. Ask for it in writing. It costs nothing, it takes one conversation, and it converts an abstract question into a figure you can insure against. We will give you an itemised quote with no obligation whatsoever — families who plan ahead make our job easier, and theirs enormously easier.

Then add what the quote does not include

A funeral home’s invoice covers what a funeral home provides. A family’s outlay is reliably larger, and the gap is where cover falls short.

  • The cemetery or crematorium

    Grave purchase and opening fees are set by the municipality or the private cemetery, not by us, and they vary widely across the Cape metro. A grave in a family plot costs something quite different to a new one.

  • Catering and the gathering

    Usually the single largest item outside the funeral itself, and the one most sensitive to numbers. Two hundred people is a different funeral to forty, whatever the coffin costs.

  • The memorial or headstone

    Frequently bought months later, and frequently unfunded. Decide now whether it sits inside the cover or outside it.

  • Travel and repatriation

    Bringing family in, or taking a loved one home to the Eastern Cape or across a border, is a real cost. Some policies include a repatriation benefit; check yours before assuming.

  • Documents and small administration

    Certified copies, death certificate and related paperwork are individually minor and collectively not nothing.

  • The weeks after

    Time off work, a household suddenly short an income, unveiling costs later on. Families who allow for this are grateful they did.

Now count the lives, not just the amount

This is where most policies fail, and it fails quietly. A family policy covers named lives, each with its own benefit — often lower for children and for extended family than the headline figure. The cover you need is not one number; it is a number for each person your family would in practice be expected to bury.

Write the list honestly. Yourself and your spouse. Children, including adult children. Your parents, and your spouse’s parents. Anyone else your family would step in for, because in many South African families that expectation is real whether or not it is written down. Then check each of those people against the policy schedule, with the benefit amount next to their name.

Insuring older parents is usually where the plan strains, because entry ages and premiums move sharply after seventy. Funeral cover for parents deals with that specifically.

Sizing it, in four steps

  1. Get the quote. One written quote for the funeral you would actually hold. Not a package name — an itemised figure.
  2. Add the outside costs. Cemetery, catering, memorial, travel. Add them to the quote to get your true target.
  3. List every life. Set the target against each person, adjusting for what a funeral for that person would realistically look like.
  4. Check the escalation. A benefit that does not grow buys a smaller funeral every year. Confirm whether your cover amount escalates, or plan to review it every few years.

A last word on the temptation to round down. The cheapest policy that leaves a R15 000 shortfall does not save your family money — it moves the cost to the week they are least able to carry it, and usually onto a credit facility. Size it properly once, and it stops being a worry.

Frequently asked

Questions families ask us.

01

Is R30 000 enough funeral cover?

It depends entirely on the funeral you have in mind and on where it is held. A simple, dignified cremation service can sit well inside that figure. A traditional burial with a large gathering, catering and a headstone will not. The only way to know is to price the funeral you would actually hold — we will quote it for you at no charge.

02

Should I insure the cost of a headstone too?

It is worth deciding deliberately rather than by accident. A memorial is often bought some months after the funeral, once the immediate costs have settled, and families frequently find there is nothing left for it. Either add it to the cover amount or plan to fund it separately.

03

Is it better to have one big policy or several small ones?

Several small policies from different insurers are harder to keep track of and easier to let lapse, and each carries its own waiting period and its own fine print. One policy sized correctly is usually simpler. The exception is deliberate: a second policy taken to top up cover for one particular life, such as an older parent.

04

Can I have too much funeral cover?

You can pay for more than you need. The funeral class is capped at R100 000 per life, and above roughly the cost of a good funeral the extra premium is usually better spent on life cover, which pays more for the same money over a longer horizon. Funeral cover vs life insurance sets out where that line sits.
