Funeral cover

Comparing funeral cover:
what actually differs.

Below the advertising, licensed policies differ on six things. Compare those — on identical cover, for identical lives — and the shortlist writes itself.

There is no best funeral policy, and anyone who names one has not asked who you are covering. Every licensed policy already owes you the same legal minimum. What genuinely differs is who they will accept, what each life is worth, how the premium behaves over twenty years, and what happens the month a debit order fails.

Why we will not rank them

Two reasons, and we would rather state them plainly than pretend to a neutrality we do not have.

The first is legal. Recommending a financial product is regulated work, and we are undertakers, not a licensed financial services provider. The second is that a ranking would be false. The insurer that is sharpest for a couple in their forties is frequently the one that will not accept a 79-year-old parent at all. The “best” policy flips on the age of the oldest life you need covered.

What we can tell you — because we deal with the claims end of this business every week — is which clauses decide how a policy behaves when it is finally used.

Compare the floor first, then the extras

Before any of the above, confirm the seller is licensed. Every licensed policy already gives you a waiting period for natural death of no more than six months, immediate cover for accidental death, credit for a waiting period already served elsewhere when you switch within 31 days, and 31 days to cancel a new policy and have your premiums returned.

A seller who cannot meet those is not offering you a worse deal. They are very probably not licensed at all, and no amount of comparison shopping helps you afterwards. Check the licence with the Financial Sector Conduct Authority on 0800 110 443 or at fsca.co.za, and read how to verify a policy before you sign anything.

Only then look at the extras — grocery benefits, airtime, tombstone contributions, repatriation, cash-back after a number of claim-free years. Several are genuinely valuable, particularly repatriation if your family would take a loved one home to another province. But an extra is worth exactly nothing on a policy that has lapsed or that excludes the life you needed covered.

The three-quote method

  1. Set the benefit from a real price. Get a written funeral quote first, and add the costs outside it. Do not insure a round number. How much cover you need works this through.
  2. List every life and every age. Premiums are priced per life and per age band. This list, not the brochure, is what you are actually buying.
  3. Get three quotes for identical cover. Same benefit, same lives, same ages. Anything else is comparing adverts.
  4. Compare the six clauses before the premium. The cheapest policy that lapses easily, or that excludes your mother, pays exactly nothing.
  5. Verify the licence. Insurer name and FSP number, checked with the FSCA. Every time, without exception.

The best funeral cover in South Africa is a licensed policy, correctly sized, on the right lives, that is still in force on the day it is needed. The quotes give you a shortlist. The method gives you the policy.

One thing to check that has nothing to do with price

Ask each insurer what happens if your family wants to use a funeral home of their own choosing. The answer will be a cash benefit, a cession, a reduced cash-in-lieu amount, or a flat no — and it is not usually on the brochure. It is, however, the clause your family will care most about. Frequently asked

Questions families ask us.

01

Which funeral cover is best in South Africa?

There is no single best policy, because the answer depends on who you are covering. The insurer that is cheapest for a family of four in their forties is rarely the one that will accept a 79-year-old mother. That is why this guide gives you a method rather than a ranking: three quotes, identical benefit, identical lives.

02

Why won't you recommend a specific insurer?

Because we are a funeral home, not a licensed financial services provider, and recommending financial products is regulated work that we are not licensed to do. We also take no commission from any insurer — which is precisely what lets us tell you the unvarnished truth about how a policy behaves when it is claimed on.

03

Are cheap funeral policies worth it?

A low advertised premium usually buys one life with modest cover, not the family plan most people picture. Cheap is fine when the premium-to-benefit ratio holds up for your real family composition and the insurer is licensed. Treat an unlicensed bargain as the most expensive policy you will ever buy.

04

How many quotes should I get?

Three, for exactly the same benefit amount and exactly the same list of insured lives. Anything else is comparing advertisements. Pricing is set per life and per age band, so changing the lives between quotes changes which insurer looks cheapest.
